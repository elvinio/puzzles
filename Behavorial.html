Behavioral Interview Prep

Answers drafted in STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
Question 18

Tell me about a time you worked effectively across a large timezone gap.

This is a template answer with placeholder specifics in italic yellow — swap those for your own team, project, and numbers before using it in an interview.
Situation

I was working on [project/initiative] with teammates split between [your region, e.g. Singapore] and [counterpart region, e.g. the US West Coast], a gap of roughly [N] hours with almost no working-hours overlap. Decisions and reviews kept stalling because everything defaulted to "let's discuss it live," which meant a one-line question could cost a full day's turnaround.

Task

I needed to keep [project] moving on schedule without relying on both sides being awake at the same time, and without burning out either team with off-hours meetings.

Action
  • Made written updates the default: every handoff included context (what, why, current blockers, and the specific decision or input needed) so the other side could act without waiting for a live conversation.
  • Kept a single shared doc ([e.g. Notion/Confluence page]) as the source of truth for decisions and open questions, instead of letting context live in chat threads or people's heads.
  • Recorded short async video walkthroughs ([e.g. Loom]) for anything visual — demos, design reviews — instead of scheduling a call.
  • Negotiated one narrow "golden hours" window per week for the few things that genuinely needed real-time discussion, and rotated who took the inconvenient time slot so the cost wasn't always on one region.
  • Set clear async SLAs (e.g. same-day response within each team's working hours) so people knew what to expect instead of chasing replies.
Result

[Project] shipped [on time / ahead of schedule], and average time-to-unblock on cross-region questions dropped from [e.g. 1 day] to [e.g. a few hours]. The async-first habits we set up were adopted by [other teams/the wider org], and teammates on both sides reported feeling more included since decisions no longer defaulted to whoever was awake first.
